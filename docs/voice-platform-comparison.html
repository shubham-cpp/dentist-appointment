Twilio and Telnyx voice platforms with GPT-5.6 Luna

This report compares Twilio ConversationRelay, a custom Telnyx TeXML pipeline, and Telnyx AI Assistant. Each path can use GPT-5.6 Luna.

Scope: inbound US dental receptionistCurrency: USDEvidence: Twilio, Telnyx, and OpenAI primary sourcesTelnyx research notes: Markdown source log

Use Telnyx AI Assistant with Luna and Ultra for the first pilot

The three choices do not compare different language models. They compare who operates speech and the real-time voice loop.

Use the same Luna prompt and tool contracts. Use one Ultra voice for both Telnyx paths and document Twilio's selected voice.

What stays the same

Luna writes the response. Your backend remains the scheduling authority.

What Telnyx changes

Custom TeXML exposes raw audio. Assistant manages the complete voice loop.

Where Twilio fits

ConversationRelay manages speech while your application keeps Luna, tools, prompts, and state.

Important correction.

"Native Luna" is a managed Assistant model option. Luna is not a Telnyx voice. Ultra is the TTS model.

Telnyx does not say that it runs Luna on Telnyx-owned GPUs. Its pricing page separates hosted Kimi from managed OpenAI models.

How the three runtime paths work

ConversationRelay manages speech but leaves the agent loop in Brightview. The two Telnyx paths place that boundary elsewhere.

Twilio ConversationRelay + Luna

Twilio owns speech. Your application owns the agent.

  1. Twilio answers the call. TwiML connects the call to ConversationRelay.
  2. Twilio transcribes speech. It sends prompts and turn events through your WebSocket.
  3. Your app calls Luna. It owns prompts, history, tools, retries, and safety checks.
  4. Your app streams text. Send Luna tokens to Twilio as they arrive.
  5. Twilio synthesizes speech. Select Google, Amazon, or ElevenLabs voices.
  6. Twilio handles interruptions. Your app receives interruption and session events.

TeXML + Ultra + Luna

Your application owns the live conversation.

  1. Telnyx answers the call. TeXML starts a bidirectional <Stream>.
  2. Your WebSocket receives audio. Your code decodes, buffers, and transcribes it.
  3. Your endpointing logic closes the turn. Your code decides when the caller stopped.
  4. Your app calls Luna. It owns prompts, history, tool calls, retries, and safety checks.
  5. Your app calls Ultra. Ultra is REST-only on the public TTS API.
  6. Your app returns audio. It queues MP3 or RTP and clears it during interruption.

Assistant + managed Luna + Ultra

Telnyx owns the live conversation runtime.

  1. Telnyx answers the call. TeXML <AIAssistant> or Call Control attaches the Assistant.
  2. Telnyx transcribes audio. You select a supported STT model and turn settings.
  3. Telnyx calls Luna. Select openai/gpt-5.6-luna without a separate OpenAI key.
  4. Telnyx runs tools. Webhook, transfer, hangup, DTMF, handoff, and MCP tools are built in.
  5. Telnyx synthesizes speech. Select the same Telnyx.Ultra.* voice.
  6. Telnyx handles turns. The runtime manages playback, interruption, and conversation records.
ConversationRelay is the middle option.

It preserves Brightview's Luna and tool loop without requiring custom STT, TTS, or raw-audio playback.

Failure behavior worth testing

ConversationRelay does not reconnect an unexpected WebSocket failure. The action callback can return new TwiML and start another connection.

TeXML can reconnect media by default. Your code must prevent duplicate audio, stale state, and repeated tool writes.

The Assistant removes Brightview's WebSocket from the speech loop. Brightview still owns tool timeouts, idempotency, and booking recovery.

Sources: ConversationRelay WebSocket messages and TeXML Stream.

Ultra's sub-100ms claim covers synthesis, not the whole turn.

STT finalization, Luna, tools, transport, and playback still affect the caller's wait.

Why Ultra's REST-only limit matters

Ultra has no public TTS WebSocket. A custom media agent must request speech over REST.

The app must then return MP3 or compatible RTP to the call. It must also clear queued audio during barge-in.

The managed Assistant hides this integration. Telnyx can still use Ultra within its Voice AI runtime.

ConversationRelay telemetry and healthcare caveat

Voice Insights separates network, STT, application, and TTS latency. It also records turns, interruptions, tokens, and errors.

Twilio states that ConversationRelay Insights is not HIPAA eligible. Do not enable it for protected health information without approval.

Source: ConversationRelay Voice Insights.

Decision guide

Choose by operating model. Do not choose by the word "native" or by the voice name.

Choose Twilio ConversationRelay when

  • You need Brightview to own Luna, prompts, tools, and state.
  • You do not want to build STT, TTS, or raw-audio playback.
  • Existing Twilio numbers, Studio flows, contracts, or staff knowledge matter.
  • Voice Insights provides useful non-PHI debugging data.
  • The higher public per-minute rate fits the operating budget.

Choose Telnyx AI Assistant when

  • You need the fastest safe production path.
  • You want managed turn-taking and interruption.
  • You want Ultra without custom audio playback code.
  • Webhook or MCP tools fit the scheduling integration.
  • Assistant versions and built-in traces reduce rollout work.

Choose custom TeXML when

  • You need raw audio or a custom turn-taking algorithm.
  • You must own exact prompts, history, and tool execution.
  • You need carrier or speech-provider portability.
  • You can operate WebSockets, audio queues, and recovery.
  • Measured savings exceed engineering and support costs.

Recommended pilot

Configure Telnyx AI Assistant with openai/gpt-5.6-luna, an Ultra voice, and Brightview webhook tools.

Run the same scenarios through ConversationRelay and custom TeXML. Keep Luna, prompt intent, tools, callers, and test scripts equal.

Measure completed appointment tasks, p95 voice latency, interruption recovery, and actual cost. Choose the lowest-operations path that passes.

Safety boundary for appointment changes

No carrier or language model should own appointment truth. Brightview must validate identity, availability, provider, and appointment type.

Brightview must also enforce idempotency. A repeated model or webhook call must not create two bookings.
